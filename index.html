Observation & Case Studies

Naturalistic Observation: Recording behavior in natural environments without intervention.
Strength: High ecological validity. Risk: Observer bias & Hawthorne Effect.

Case Study: In-depth investigation of an individual, group, or rare phenomenon.
Strength: Rich qualitative data. Risk: Low generalizability.

Ecological Validity
Real-world natural setting

Surveys & Interviews

Surveys: Self-report questionnaires administered to large samples.
Strength: Rapid data collection. Risk: Social desirability bias & response bias.

Interviews: Structured or semi-structured face-to-face dialogues.
Strength: Nuanced insight. Risk: Interviewer bias & time intensive.

Sample Representation
Large-scale quantitative reach

Correlational Studies

Correlational Research: Statistical measurement of the relationship between two naturally occurring variables (Correlation Coefficient: r from -1.0 to +1.0).

Golden Rule: Correlation does NOT imply causation! Third variables (confounding factors) may explain the link.

Causality Protection
Identifies trends, not cause-and-effect

Method Matching & Bias Mitigation Matrix

Research Method Best Applied When... Primary Strengths Limitations & Key Biases Control / Mitigation Strategy
Naturalistic Obs. Studying spontaneous behaviors in real-world settings. High realism; uninfluenced by lab settings. Hawthorne Effect (acting differently when watched); Observer Bias. Unobtrusive observation (hidden cameras or cameras at distance).
Case Study Investigating rare neurological conditions or unique situations. Deeply detailed analysis; generates future hypotheses. Cannot generalize to general population; Subjective interpretation. Triangulate data using multiple testing instruments.
Surveys & Polls Gathering self-reported attitudes across large groups quickly. Cost-effective; easy to quantify statistically. Social Desirability Bias (lying to look good); Response Rate Bias. Ensure complete participant anonymity & neutral wording.
Correlational Evaluating relationships where experimental manipulation is unethical. Predicts outcomes; allows study of non-manipulable variables. Directionality problem; Third-Variable (Confounding) Problem. Use longitudinal tracking and partial correlation analysis.
